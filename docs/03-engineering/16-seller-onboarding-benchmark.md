# Seller Onboarding Benchmark — How Top Marketplaces Run It End-to-End

> **Project:** VinTech Challenge 2026
>
> **Version:** 1.0
>
> **Owner:** Engineering Team
>
> **Status:** Report

> **Scope note:** This report is the deliverable of tracker ticket #102 (research, part of #98), reframed to the broad question the project owner asked: how the top-league marketplaces (Mercari, eBay, Vinted, Amazon, OfferUp, Etsy, Facebook Marketplace) run seller onboarding end-to-end, and the concrete pattern the Used Goods Marketplace should copy. It feeds the seller-onboarding design (become-a-seller, verification ladder, trust score, verified filter) and the T21 mock-Fayda work (ADR-020). Every claim cites its source (name + URL) inline; findings were gathered from official help centers, seller handbooks, and platform engineering/legal docs.

# 1. Purpose and method

The question has six parts: (1) signup & friction, (2) the verification ladder (tiers, when each is required, what each unlocks), (3) trust signals and reputation, (4) trust/verification technology and pricing, (5) anti-abuse (limits, holds, ban evasion), (6) a synthesis for a VinTech 2026 hackathon demo (Next.js + Supabase, Ethiopian users, unreliable SMS OTP, mock Fayda national-ID OIDC, 5-minute judge demo).

Method: primary sources only where possible — official help-center articles, Seller Central / Seller Hub docs, platform legal terms, and the platforms' own engineering or transparency publications. Secondary write-ups are used only for pricing intelligence (vendor rates are largely negotiated) and for the one stat (eBay new-seller hold) where the platform's current help page describes the mechanism but third parties report the numbers. Platform references are as of Aug 2026; fees/limits drift, so verify live before implementation.

The headline finding: **the top league does not verify sellers before they can list.** Every one of the seven platforms lets a new user list within minutes of signup and defers identity verification to a later, higher-value moment (first payout, higher price ceiling, high-risk category, or suspicion). Verification is a *progressive ladder that unlocks privileges*, not a gate on the core loop — and where a platform did force verification early (Amazon, and Etsy's 2025 mandatory re-verification), it treats that as the price of a professional/business seller tier, with heavier revenue to justify it. The platforms that compete on casual, C2C volume (Mercari, Vinted, Facebook Marketplace, OfferUp) keep the entry bar near zero.

# 2. Signup & friction — selling is enabled at signup or within minutes

- **Mercari:** you register and can begin listing immediately; the profile-verification badge (email + SMS + government ID) is explicitly optional and "not required" (Mercari, "Verification and Badges", https://www.mercari.com/us/help_center/topics/account/guides/verification-and-badges/). Phone number is collected *during signup*, so the low tier is effectively free. No seller application, no upfront ID.
- **Vinted:** listing is instant; payment is what triggers identity work. To get paid you must "activate your Vinted Wallet" (name, birth date, address, partial SSN and/or a passport/ID/licence copy), and if you haven't verified it in advance you must do so **within 5 business days of your first sale or the order is cancelled** (Vinted, "Why you may be asked for your ID", https://www.vinted.com/help/15/111-why-you-may-be-asked-for-your-id). So: list free, verify at first payout.
- **eBay:** registration now confirms identity at signup (name, nationality, address, date of birth; a photo ID "in some cases") plus bank details for payouts — but this is the *seller-account* step, still friction-light: no document upload required for most individuals unless eBay emails a request (eBay, "Registering as a seller", https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/getting-paid/registering-seller?id=4792).
- **Facebook Marketplace:** no seller signup at all — any Facebook account can list. Government-ID verification is only required to unlock *shipping* ("why you need to verify your identity when you set up shipping"), and local pickup sells with no ID at all (Meta, "Acceptable forms of identification for seller verification", https://www.facebook.com/help/813575322878694).
- **OfferUp:** TruYou (phone + ID + selfie, via Onfido) is free and "completely voluntary (in most cases)" — you can sell without it. It becomes mandatory only if OfferUp flags unusual/fraud-like activity, at which point the account is *locked until TruYou is approved* (OfferUp, "About TruYou", https://help.offerup.com/hc/en-us/articles/360031993072-About-TruYou).
- **Amazon (the outlier, and why):** a professional seller register goes through 5 steps — business info, seller info, billing, store, **verify identity** — and you can't list until verified. Verification needs a government-issued ID + a 180-day address proof, then either an instant face/ID photo check or a video call, and takes "about three business days" (Amazon, "How to register as an Amazon seller", https://sell.amazon.com/sell/registration-guide; Amazon registration guides PDF, https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/02/sell/images/Registration_Guide_to_Amazon_US_UK.pdf). Amazon charges ~$40/month and targets business sellers, so its funnel buys the higher bar.
- **Etsy (partially an outlier):** identity verification via Persona is **required to set up a shop** — ID + selfie, biometric or manual review (Etsy, "How to Verify Your Identity on Etsy", https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/22481159004567-How-to-Verify-Your-Identity-on-Etsy). In June 2025 Etsy extended this to mandatory re-verification of *existing* sellers, with payment holds and listing suspension as the penalty for not complying (Etsy Seller Policy change, reported https://skladusa.com/en/blog/etsy-mandatory-account-verification/).

**The conversion cost of forcing verification early.** This is well quantified, and it is the reason the top league defers:

- **68%** of consumers have abandoned a digital onboarding at least once because verification was too slow, too intrusive, or asked for documents they didn't have; abandonment rose from 40% (2016) to 63% (2020) to 68% (2022). The average user bails at **18 min 53 s**, and **38%** abandoned specifically for lack of the required document (Signicat, *Battle to Onboard*, via https://oneid.uk/news-and-events/identity-verification-abandonment-cost).
- Push verification past **3 minutes and abandonment climbs to ~70%**; asking a user to *re-upload* a document makes them ~3× more likely to quit (https://bankingwith.com/articles/kyc-verification-process-does-strict/).
- Marketplace/e-commerce onboarding abandonment runs **60–70%**; the primary friction point is "seller verification and payment integration" (Jumio, https://www.jumio.com/how-to-reduce-customer-abandonment/).
- Monzo's US signup is the cleanest case study: one specific check was responsible for **40% of drop-off**; deferring it for low-risk users (risk-bucketing via Alloy) took signup completion from **9% to 40%** and cut median time from 17 to 7 minutes (Monzo engineering blog, https://monzo.com/blog/how-we-cut-time-to-sign-up-for-monzo-us-from-17-minutes-to-4-minutes). The lesson: a single forced early check can halve the funnel; a ladder that only burdens high-risk or high-value users multiplies it.
- The mechanism the signup data points to: identity checks that **require no physical document** (OAuth/digital identity, bank or mobile-network-based proof) "remove this barrier entirely" and complete in seconds (Signicat, via https://oneid.uk/news-and-events/identity-verification-abandonment-cost). This is the strongest argument for Fayda OIDC over document upload in this project.

# 3. The verification ladder — tiers, when required, what unlocks

The universal shape across platforms is a **ladder of tiers, each unlocked by evidence of increasing strength, each gating higher-value actions**. The strongest evidence (government ID + selfie) is almost never a signup requirement; it gates payouts, high price ceilings, or trust badges.

**Tier 0 — account (email):** baseline everywhere. Etsy adds it; Mercari collects phone at signup (Mercari, "Verification and Badges", above).

**Tier 1 — phone/SMS:** Mercari counts a verified SMS toward its badge and uses 2FA (Mercari, above). OfferUp's "Confirmed Phone" is a standalone profile badge (OfferUp, "About profile badges", https://help.offerup.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032335411-About-profile-badges). Amazon verifies the seller's phone with an SMS/call PIN during registration (Amazon registration guide, above). Phone verification is cheap and is the first gate.

**Tier 2 — government ID (+ selfie) as the "verified" tier:**
- **Mercari:** the Verified badge = email + SMS + government ID (upload ID + selfie, verified against each other, up to 48 h, and **a valid credit/debit card on file** is required). It is optional. The same ID check is used as a *financial* verification: Mercari is a regulated Money Service Business and must verify identity (name, street address, DOB, SSN) against a national database for transactions — at thresholds, not at signup (Mercari, "Why do I need to verify my identity?", https://www.mercari.com/us/help_center/article/369/; "Mercari's price limit", https://www.mercari.com/us/help_center/article/515/). Price ceiling is **$2,000 per listing normally, $5,000 only after identity verification** for Authenticate items (https://www.mercari.com/us/help_center/article/515/).
- **OfferUp:** TruYou = phone + two ID photos + on-the-spot selfie via Onfido, which checks document authenticity and selfie-to-ID match; badge is permanent and "cannot be removed" (OfferUp, "TruYou verification program", https://help.offerup.com/hc/en-us/articles/360031993072-TruYou-verification-program).
- **eBay:** individuals normally give PII (name, nationality, address, DOB); eBay requests a photo ID document only "in some cases", uploaded as JPEG/PNG/PDF ≤4 MB (eBay, "Registering as a seller", above). EU DSA adds mandatory trader traceability for business sellers: ID document or eID, bank details, trade-register number, self-certification (eBay Ireland User Agreement update, https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-ireland-user-agreement-update-digital-services-act/).
- **Vinted:** the wallet-activation step collects name/DOB/address/partial SSN and may require an ID copy; higher sales thresholds (set by Adyen) trigger tier-2 KYC document uploads (passport, licence, or residence permit), and a still-higher tier adds a bank statement; suspicious activity jumps you up the tiers (Vinted, "Why you may be asked for your ID", https://www.vinted.com/help/15/111-why-you-may-be-asked-for-your-id).
- **Amazon:** ID + address proof at registration (above); high-volume and re-verified sellers go through additional checks.
- **Etsy:** Persona ID + selfie, mandatory at shop creation (above).
- **Facebook Marketplace:** ID only for shipping; INFORM Act applies to high-volume sellers (Meta, above).

**Tier 3 — payment/bank verification, tied to payout:**
- **Vinted:** wallet KYC by first sale +5 days (above). **Mercari:** ID validated against a national database; payouts (Instant Pay) explicitly require verified profile — "Verification is your key to accessing helpful features like Instant Pay" (Mercari, "Verification and Badges", above).
- **eBay:** bank account verified via online banking credentials, microdeposits, or a statement; payouts blocked until verified (eBay, "Registering as a seller", above).
- **Amazon:** bank/deposit method + tax interview required before listing (Amazon registration guide, above). **OfferUp:** deposit account via Stripe; payments pause until Stripe-required data (SSN/ITIN, address) is validated (OfferUp, "Personal data required for in-app payments", https://help.offerup.com/hc/en-us/articles/360031995012-Personal-data-required-for-in-app-payments).
- **Etsy:** bank account via Plaid (US) at shop creation; taxpayer ID (SSN/EIN) by $500 in sales or the shop is suspended (Etsy, "How to Verify Your Seller Information for Etsy Payments", https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001980067-How-to-Verify-Your-Seller-Information-for-Etsy-Payments).

**Tier 4 — regulatory thresholds (triggered, not opt-in):** the US INFORM Consumers Act and EU DSA force ID + tax-ID + bank collection once a seller crosses thresholds. Mercari: 200 transactions + $5,000 in a year → full verification within 10 days or selling is suspended; $20,000/year → name/address disclosed to buyers (Mercari, "INFORM Consumers Law", https://www.mercari.com/us/help_center/article/598/). OfferUp is identical (200 items / $5,000, via Stripe, 10-day deadline) (OfferUp, "The INFORM Consumers Act", https://help.offerup.com/hc/en-us/articles/24130900536340-The-INFORM-Consumers-Act). Facebook: high-volume sellers paused while info is verified, ~2 business days (Meta, "About federal regulations", https://www.facebook.com/help/3559483297614032/). Vinted reports UK sellers to HMRC at 30 sales or €2,000/yr (Vinted, "HMRC requirements", https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1149-hmrc-requirements-what-you-need-to-know).

**Summary of "when is each required":** email/phone at signup or first list; government ID when *something valuable is at stake* — first payout, >$2k price, shipping, high-volume status, or a risk flag; payment verification at first payout; regulatory KYC only at volume thresholds. Nothing is required to post a first low-value listing.

# 4. Trust signals & reputation — how "verified" is displayed and earned

- **Mercari:** the Verified badge shows on the profile; explicitly "Our search algorithm favors verified sellers" — verification is a **ranking lever, not just a badge** (Mercari, "Verification and Badges", above). Mercari also shows Pro Seller / Top Rated badges and a ratings+review count next to listings (observed in Mercari's search API data shape, https://explainx.ai/skills/mercari.com/search-listings-4ixl7y/search-listings). To earn the badge: email + SMS + government ID (three verifications), all done in the Trust & Verification section of the profile (Mercari, above).
- **OfferUp:** a full *badge system* tied to verifiable actions — Confirmed Phone, Confirmed Email, Facebook Connect, Reply Rate (earned by responding fast), and the TruYou badge "added to your profile photo". Badges are the platform's stated reputation mechanism: "A simple way to build your reputation is by earning profile badges" (OfferUp, "About profile badges", above; "About your reputation", https://help.offerup.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032336491-About-your-reputation).
- **eBay:** no identity "verified" badge for consumers; trust is the **feedback score** plus the earned **Top Rated Seller** badge (active 90+ days, 100+ transactions and $1,000+ in the past year, defect rate ≤0.5%, ≤3% late shipments, tracking for 95%, cases closed without seller resolution ≤0.3%). The badge grants **prominence in Best Match search** and fee discounts (eBay seller-performance criteria via https://www.edesk.com/blog/ebay-top-rated-seller/; academic write-up of the badge's effect, https://www.ama.org/2026/04/21/when-a-trust-badge-changes-what-sellers-do/). A key finding from that research: detailed seller ratings on profile pages were viewed by **<1% of buyers** — the badge works because it sits on the search-result page, in the main decision path.
- **Amazon:** sellers earn trust from ratings/feedback and, for item authenticity, the Authenticity/Verified program; the "Amazon Verified Profile" badge is reserved for verified brand/product representatives (e.g., authors, manufacturers), not generic sellers (Amazon, "Badges", https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GED7RL944YMQ8CE3).
- **Etsy:** the **Star Seller** badge (95% message response within 24 h, 95% on-time shipping with tracking, ≥4.8 average rating, 5 orders + $300 in the trailing 3 months, 90 days after first sale) — reviewed monthly, shown on the shop, and it is a *search/visibility* lever: failing to maintain it may impact search visibility (Etsy, "What is the Star Seller Badge?", https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/4403058372503-What-is-the-Star-Seller-Badge).
- **Vinted:** no seller identity badge for regular sellers; trust is feedback/reviews plus the **Item Verification** service — an optional, buyer-paid (~€10) authenticity check that runs high-value designer items through a trained in-house verification hub (Hamburg) and stamps the listing with a diamond badge (Vinted, "Item Verification for designer items", https://www.vinted.com/help/1147-item-verification-for-designer-items; Retail Week, https://www.retail-week.com/marketplaces/vinted-launches-verification-service-for-designer-items/7044974.article).
- **Facebook Marketplace:** seller badges (earned from good selling history) show only on the Marketplace profile; the old "verified profile" Marketplace badge was **discontinued** — verification there is now about identity for shipping and INFORM compliance, not a displayed trust mark (Meta, "Get seller badges on Facebook", https://www.facebook.com/help/1684084458520855/; "What is a verified profile on Facebook Marketplace?", https://www.facebook.com/help/2271759806184961).

**Pattern to copy:** verification must be (a) displayed in the decision path (search results, listing cards), (b) multi-signal (identity + behavior + ratings), and (c) worth earning — a search-ranking boost like Mercari's, not a neutral flag.

# 5. Trust/verification technology & vendors

- **IDV vendors actually used by these platforms:** Etsy uses **Persona** (ID + selfie, biometric or manual review; identity doc and selfie retained 6 years after shop close, biometrics ≤1 year) (Etsy, "Identity Verification with Persona on Etsy", https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/22504854625815-Identity-Verification-with-Persona-on-Etsy). OfferUp uses **Onfido** (ID authenticity + selfie match) (OfferUp, "About TruYou", above) and **Stripe** for payout-side identity/tax (OfferUp, above). Vinted outsources KYC to **Adyen / Mangopay / Vinted Pay**, which run the tiered checks (Vinted, above). Amazon runs in-house + third-party document/forgery/liveness tech and human video calls (Amazon registration guides, above). Mercari checks identity against **national databases** (Mercari, "Why do I need to verify my identity?", above). So the market-standard stack is: document+selfie liveness IDV (Persona/Onfido/Jumio/Stripe Identity), national-database checks, and regulated PSPs for the payout-tier KYC.
- **Rough pricing (all vendor rates are negotiated; these are list/blended figures):** **Stripe Identity** is the only published one — $1.50 per completed verification (first 50 free), ~$0.50 for an ID-number/SSN validation (Stripe pricing, https://stripe.com/pricing; https://stripe.com/identity). **Persona**: Essential plan $250/month incl. 500 free services, then $1.50/service; typically $1–2 per government-ID+selfie verification (Persona, "Plans Overview", https://help.withpersona.com/articles/6oZbzp7jb7AWGClF5vpY3K/; APIScout survey, https://apiscout.dev/guides/best-identity-verification-apis-2026). **Onfido**: ~$0.65–$1.25 document+selfie at mid volume (up to $2–4 at low volume), plus AML watchlist ~$0.12–$0.30 and device intelligence ~$0.05–$0.12 (https://beverified.org/providers/onfido/). **Jumio**: custom, ~$2–5/check at scale (https://apiscout.dev/guides/best-identity-verification-apis-2026). Reality check for a hackathon: even the cheapest published per-check price (~$1.50) is real money per demo verification, and Persona/Onfido/Jumio are enterprise contracts — **not viable for the demo; the mock-Fayda OIDC is the right call.**
- **Device fingerprinting / ban evasion:** purpose-built products — Fingerprint, Incognia, SEON, Sumsub (rebadged Fingerprint), Shufti — produce a persistent, cookie-independent device ID to catch multi-accounting and banned-seller returns; they sit *upstream* of KYC so low-risk users skip the heavy check (https://fingerprint.com/use-cases/new-account-fraud-prevention/; https://www.incognia.com/device-intelligence; https://shuftipro.com/device-fingerprinting/). Incognia claims an 84% reduction in new-user abandonment by keeping verification frictionless for trusted devices (https://www.incognia.com/device-intelligence).
- **Manual review** remains the fallback on every platform (Mercari up to 48 h ID review; Amazon video calls; Etsy's non-biometric option "generally takes longer"; Vinted/Adyen human document checks) — for a small marketplace, a manual admin queue is a legitimate tier, and the repo already has the `verifications` table + admin review page for it (see `14-industry-standard-audit.md` finding #3).

# 6. Anti-abuse — what gates listers early

Every platform pairs the light entry bar with **structural gates that bite as volume/value grows**, not at signup:

- **Listing limits by trust level:** Mercari caps new accounts at **100 listings, +100 per completed sale** (confirmed by a Mercari spokesperson: "Our listing limit increases as sellers complete more sales", https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2024/04/12/mercari-lifted-selling-fees-but-beware-possible-limits/). Facebook Marketplace limits **new accounts to ~1 listing/day for ~30 days** while they build history (reported consistently by sellers; Meta publishes only that limits exist, https://www.facebook.com/help/811082570742714; e.g. https://shiftlysupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/50105290667283). The repo's audit already flagged the absence of global rate limits as a P1 (`14-industry-standard-audit.md` finding #18); a listing cap is the marketplace-native version.
- **Payment holds & reserves:** eBay holds new sellers' funds until delivery confirmation (or ~14 days without tracking) and may impose rolling reserves (e.g., 10% over 60 days) for riskier accounts (eBay "Transaction holds", https://www.ebay.com.au/help/selling/getting-paid/getting-paid-items-youve-sold/payments-hold?id=4816; eBay Payments Terms, https://ir.ebaystatic.com/pictures/aw/pics/payment/us-payments-terms-of-use-2025-04-21.pdf). Mercari releases funds only after the buyer rates or a 3-day auto-rate window closes (Mercari, "Ratings for Sellers", https://www.mercari.com/us/help_center/topics/ratings/guides/ratings-for-sellers/). Vinted suspends the wallet until KYC clears (Vinted, above). OfferUp pauses payments until Stripe-required data is validated (OfferUp, above). Amazon runs disbursement reserves for new sellers.
- **Risk-triggered step-up:** OfferUp *locks* an account and demands TruYou when fraud-like activity is detected (OfferUp, above); eBay verifies identity again on any account-detail change, holding payouts meanwhile (eBay, "Payment troubleshooting", https://sellercentre.ebay.com.au/managed-payments-troubleshooting); Vinted jumps to higher KYC tiers on suspicious behavior (Vinted, above).
- **Ban evasion:** the device-intelligence vendors above exist precisely to stop banned sellers re-registering; the standard integration fingerprints signup/listing actions and blocks repeat offenders (https://fingerprint.com/use-cases/new-account-fraud-prevention/).

The design implication: **let anyone list, then make the platform's protections scale with the seller's value to the platform** — listing caps and payout holds until a track record exists, identity step-up on risk signals, device fingerprinting (or, in the demo, a cheap Supabase-side heuristic like a per-device/email/phone cap) to blunt multi-account abuse.

# 7. Synthesis — what the Used Goods Marketplace should copy (VinTech 2026 demo)

**Constraint recap:** Next.js + Supabase, Ethiopian users, **SMS OTP is unreliable** (the Indian fintech data is the closest analogue: Aadhaar-linked SMS OTP delivery failure runs 15–20% and a single e-KYC OTP step loses 30–40% of users, https://productgrowth.in/insights/fintech/why-users-drop-off-before-kyc/), a **mock Fayda national-ID OIDC is available** (T21, ADR-020; see `docs/agents/research/fayda-verification-mock.md`), and the judges see **5 minutes**.

**The recommended ladder — copy Mercari's "list now, verify later" shape, but swap the SMS tier for Fayda:**

1. **Become-a-seller = zero friction (Tier 0).** One tap ("Start selling") promotes the account (`promoteToSeller` — the exact P0 gap in `14-industry-standard-audit.md` finding #1) and the seller lists immediately. Email is the only baseline. This matches Mercari/Vinted/Facebook — the core loop must not be gated. Judges see a listing live within seconds.
2. **Phone = best-effort, not blocking (Tier 1).** Because SMS is unreliable, phone verification must be *offerable but non-blocking*, with email-link and Telegram fallbacks (the repo's PRD trust framework already lists Telegram). A phone-unverified seller lists normally but is capped (see §6). This is exactly the Monzo lesson: defer the 40%-drop-off check for low-risk users (Monzo blog, above). Never make a flaky OTP a hard gate for the core action.
3. **Fayda = the government-ID tier, and it is a *login*, not an upload (Tier 2).** This is the demo's killer move and the single strongest pattern from the research: the #1 abandonment trigger is "document not in hand" (38%, Signicat via https://oneid.uk/news-and-events/identity-verification-abandonment-cost), and source-based verification (OAuth to where the data lives) recovers most of it (https://burnt.com/blog-onboarding-drop-off). Fayda OIDC is an OAuth login — the user clicks "Verify with Fayda", authenticates at the IdP, and the marketplace stores only the verified `sub` + flag (per `fayda-verification-mock.md`). Zero document photography, completes in seconds, no SMS involved. It must be **voluntary but highly rewarded** — Mercari's model, not Amazon's.
4. **Friction goes where value is (Tier 3+).** Require Fayda (or admin manual review) for: first payout/contact-unlock, listings above a price ceiling (Mercari's $2k/$5k pattern), and repeat-abuse flags. The `verifications` enum (email/phone/telegram/fayda) and `record_verification` RPC already model this; the work is making the RPC reachable and adding the self-serve Fayda write path (T21, per `fayda-verification-mock.md` §2 and `14-industry-standard-audit.md` finding #3).
5. **Trust score + verified badge as a ranking lever (Trust signals).** Keep the composite trust score (PRD FR:140-147) but make verification *matter*: **verified sellers rank higher in search** (Mercari's explicit statement) and the **verified filter** (already in PRD FS:324-331, still missing — audit finding #12) surfaces in search. Badge lives on the listing card and profile — in the decision path, not buried (the eBay <1% profile-views lesson, https://www.ama.org/2026/04/21/when-a-trust-badge-changes-what-sellers-do/).
6. **Anti-abuse for the demo:** listing cap by trust level (e.g., unverified = 5 listings, Fayda-verified = unlimited), rate limits (audit finding #18), and an admin review queue as the manual tier — all cheap in Supabase and all presentable to judges.

**Where the demo puts friction vs smoothness (the 5-minute script):** smooth — signup → list → live in ~30 s; smooth — one-click Fayda verification with the mock OIDC, badge + trust bump + ranking lift visible immediately (Shot 3/5 of `13-demo-script.md`); smooth — buyer filters "verified sellers only" and sees the badge on cards (audit finding #12, PRD FS:324-331). Friction shown deliberately — a capped, unverified seller attempting a high-price listing is asked to verify with Fayda, and the admin verification queue handles the manual fallback. That single arc demonstrates the whole ladder in under 5 minutes and mirrors exactly how Mercari/eBay/Vinted earn trust without strangling volume.
