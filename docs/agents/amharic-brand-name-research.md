## Purpose

Research Amharic (Ethiopian) words, naming conventions, and cultural context to identify a native-feeling brand name for a second-hand goods marketplace. The goal is a name that feels local, trustworthy, and descriptive — not a generic English translation.

## 2. Existing Ethiopian Marketplace / Classified Brands

No dominant, nationally-recognized second-hand marketplace brand was found in Ethiopia. The landscape is fragmented:

| Name | Type | Notes |
|------|------|-------|
| **Merkato (መርካቶ)** | Physical market | Africa's largest open-air market, Addis Ababa. Not a brand — a place. Dominant reference point for "market" in Ethiopia. |
| **Addis Mercato** | Physical market | Same as above; the neighborhood/district name. |
| **Ethiopian Classifieds** (ethiopianclassifieds.com) | Online classifieds | Defunct or inactive; generic English name. |
| **Reklam Ethiopia** | Advertising portal | General ads, not marketplace-specific. |
| **Jumia Ethiopia** | E-commerce (defunct) | Pan-African; exited Ethiopia. |
| **Delala** | Informal term | "Delala" (ደላላ) = broker/middleman; used for informal second-hand dealers. |
| **Terra sections** | Market zones | "Terra" (ቈርቈር?) = sections within Merkato for specific goods (furniture, electronics, scrap). |

**Key insight:** There is no strong incumbent second-hand marketplace brand in Ethiopia. The market is open for a native name.

## 3. Cultural Naming Conventions in Ethiopia

### 3.1 Personal Names (Habesha Convention)
- **No family names.** Ethiopians use patronymics: given name + father's name (+ grandfather's name).
- **Names have meanings.** Virtue names (Abebe = "he flowered"), religious names (Kidus = "holy"), nature names.
- **Multigenerational wordplay.** A father named Abebe ("blossomed") might name his son Kidus ("holy") — creating poetic lineage.

### 3.2 Business Naming Patterns
Ethiopian businesses typically use one of these patterns:

1. **Personal name + service** — "Abebe Grocery," "Tilahun Electronics"
2. **Descriptive Amharic word** — "Merkato," "Gebeya"
3. **Aspirational/positive word** — "Selam" (peace), "Imnet" (trust)
4. **Location-based** — "Bole" (neighborhood), "Merkato"
5. **Modern/foreign loanwords** — "Plaza," "Center," "Mart"

### 3.3 What Makes a Name Feel "Ethiopian" vs Generic

| Feels Ethiopian | Feels Generic |
|----------------|---------------|
| Uses Amharic word roots | Direct English translation |
| Evokes Merkato/Gebeya imagery | "Ethiopian Second-Hand Store" |
| Short, pronounceable in Amharic | Long compound English |
| References community/trust values | "Best Deals Ethiopia" |
| Uses Ge'ez script (ፊደል) | Latin-only |
| Connects to coffee/heritage culture | Generic e-commerce terms |

### 3.4 Phonetic Considerations
- Amharic speakers use ejective consonants (p', t', k', ts', ch') — hard for foreigners.
- Brand names should avoid ejectives if targeting international users.
- Favor open syllables: CV (consonant-vowel) structure.
- Avoid consonant clusters that don't exist in Amharic.

## 5. Cultural Context: What Makes a Name Feel "Ethiopian"

### 5.1 Positive Connotations to Leverage
- **Community (Mahber/Mahbereseb)** — Ethiopians value collective identity.
- **Trust (Imnet)** — Essential in informal markets where quality varies.
- **Heritage (Qirs)** — Ethiopia's ancient civilization (Aksum, Lalibela) gives pride in heritage.
- **Coffee (Buna)** — Coffee ceremony is central to social and business life.
- **Peace (Selam)** — "Selam" is both greeting and aspiration.
- **Habesha identity** — Pan-Ethiopian/Eritrean cultural pride.

### 5.2 Negative Connotations to Avoid
- **"Aroge" (old)** — Can imply worn-out, not vintage. Use carefully.
- **"Qors" (second-hand, colloquial)** — Slightly derogatory; implies cheap/used-up.
- **"Delala" (broker/middleman)** — Can imply exploitative middleman.
- **"Shekole" (lie/false)** — Obviously avoid.
- **Overly religious terms** — Ethiopia is multi-faith (Orthodox Christian, Muslim, Protestant, traditional). Avoid sectarian names.

### 5.3 The "Merkato" Factor
- Merkato is the mental model for "marketplace" in Ethiopia.
- Any name evoking Merkato will resonate.
- But Merkato is also chaotic, overwhelming, unorganized — a second-hand marketplace might want to differentiate as "organized Merkato" or "trusted Merkato."

### 5.4 Script Considerations
- **Ge'ez script (ፊደል)** is a strong brand differentiator.
- A logo using Amharic characters signals "this is ours."
- But ensure Latin transliteration works for diaspora and international users.
- Consider dual-script branding: መርካቶ / Merkato.

## 6. Recommendations

### 6.1 Top Pick: **Liwwut** (ልውውጥ)
- **Why:** Directly means "exchange/swap" — the core action of a second-hand marketplace.
- **Pros:** Unique, not generic like "Merkato." Easy to pronounce. No negative connotations. Works in Latin and Ge'ez.
- **Cons:** Less immediately recognizable than Merkato; requires brand-building.

### 6.2 Runner-Up: **Waga** (ዋጋ)
- **Why:** Means "price/value" — the primary concern of second-hand buyers.
- **Pros:** Extremely short (2 syllables). Memorable. Works globally. Positive connotation.
- **Cons:** Less descriptive of the marketplace concept; could be confused with a pricing tool.

### 6.3 Safe Choice: **Merkato** (መርካቶ)
- **Why:** Instantly understood by every Ethiopian. The mental model for "market."
- **Pros:** Zero explanation needed. Strong brand potential.
- **Cons:** Generic — may need a modifier (e.g., "Mekina Merkato" for cars, "Liwwut Merkato" for exchange).

### 6.4 Aspirational Choice: **Imnet** (እምነ቉)
- **Why:** Trust is the #1 barrier in second-hand markets. Naming the platform "Trust" signals safety.
- **Pros:** Emotional resonance. Differentiates from chaotic Merkato experience.
- **Cons:** Abstract; doesn't convey "marketplace" directly.

## 7. Names to Avoid Copying

| Name | Reason |
|------|--------|

| **Addis Ababa** | City name; not a marketplace. |
| **Habesha** (alone) | Politically charged; may alienate non-Amhara Ethiopians (Oromo, Somali, Tigray, etc.). |
| **Jumia** | Defunct in Ethiopia; associated with failure. |
| **Ethiopian Classifieds** | Generic English; defunct site. |
